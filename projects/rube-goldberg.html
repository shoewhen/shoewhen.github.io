Rube Goldberg
Machine

One stage of a 15-person, multi-stage machine, built around sensor-triggered motors and an Arduino-controlled elevator that sends the ball back for a second run.

MECHANICAL SYSTEM

OVERVIEW

One machine, many handoffs

For EGR121 Design Challenge 4, the class built a single multi-stage Rube Goldberg machine as a 15-person team. Each small group designed one "SportBot" stage that had to take the ball from the stage before it and pass it reliably to the stage after it. Every stage had to combine mechanical motion with its own sensor-triggered electronics.

My group of three built a stage with two circuits: a simple electromechanical gate, and an Arduino-controlled elevator that carried the ball back to the top so the machine could run a second time.

HOW OUR STAGE WORKS

Seesaw, gate, elevator, finale

The ball drops onto a seesaw and tips it. A tilt sensor on the seesaw closes a circuit that powers a motor, which swings a gear-shaped gate open. Once the gate has turned far enough, it presses a limit switch that cuts the motor, and the ball rolls down the seesaw toward the elevator.

At the base of the elevator, an ultrasonic sensor detects the ball. After a five-second delay, the Arduino runs the lift motor for seven seconds to raise the ball. On its way back down, the ball passes three more ultrasonic sensors, and each one adds a line to a message on a 16×2 LCD screen as the ball goes by.

MY CONTRIBUTION

The elevator return

I led the design and integration of the elevator. The lift motor is switched by a MOSFET driven from the Arduino, with a flyback diode to protect the circuit, and the sensor timing had to be tuned so the ball was fully loaded before the lift started. Because the elevator fed the ball back to the top of the new course, I also worked with the other subteams to line up handoffs and timing so the full system ran reliably from end to end.

ELECTRONICS & SUSTAINABILITY

Schematics and reusable parts

We laid out all three circuits in Tinkercad: the tilt-sensor gate circuit, the elevator motor driver, and the sensor and LCD circuit. The memo below includes the action-summary sketches and full schematics.

We built the structure from wood and 3D-printed parts printed to size, so very little material was wasted. After the demonstration we took the structure apart for reuse and returned the electronics to the Foundry for other students.
